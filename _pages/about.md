Welcome to the academic website of Tewelde Berihu, Ph.D., an atmospheric scientist and meteorologist at the Faculty of Meteorology and Hydrology, Institute of Water Technology, Arba Minch University, Ethiopia.

My research focuses on atmospheric processes, climate variability, precipitation dynamics, and climate-related applications in East Africa. I am interested in understanding the physical mechanisms that influence regional rainfall variability and in applying climate data analysis and modelling techniques to improve our understanding of weather and climate.

Research Interests

My research interests include:

Climate variability and change: Understanding climate variability across interannual, seasonal, and subseasonal timescales.

Precipitation variability and its drivers: Investigating the influence of large-scale climate modes, including ENSO, the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), the Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO), and the Quasi-Biennial Oscillation (QBO), on East African rainfall.

Seasonal climate prediction: Evaluating climate forecast products and exploring approaches to improve regional climate prediction and forecast verification.

Evapotranspiration and hydroclimatology: Assessing reference evapotranspiration, its meteorological drivers, and its implications for water resources and agricultural applications.

Climate data analysis and applications: Using gridded climate datasets, statistical methods, geospatial analysis, and programming tools to investigate climate processes and their impacts.

Research Approach

My work combines observational and gridded climate datasets with statistical analysis, climate diagnostics, and geospatial techniques. I am particularly interested in translating climate research into useful information for seasonal forecasting, water-resource management, and climate-sensitive sectors in Ethiopia and the wider East African region.

Academic and Professional Activities

At Arba Minch University, I contribute to meteorology and hydrology education and research. I am interested in scientific collaboration, student research, climate data applications, and the development of improved weather and climate information services.
